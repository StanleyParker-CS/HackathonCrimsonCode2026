Basic Form of Derivatives

  • The basic form of a derivative is as follows:

    f(x) is the original function: xn. f'(x) is the first derivative: nxn-1.

    Example: f(x) = 4x5 + 2x2 + 8. Then, f'(x) = 20x4 + 4x.

    This is called the Power Rule and is the most fundmental topic in differential calculus.

    Another way of showing the derivative of something is: d⁄dx

    Example: d⁄dx[f(x)] = f'(x)


Important Derivatives to Remember

  • Some of the derivatives seen here are important to remember and don't always conform to normal rules.


    Basic Trig Derivatives

    d⁄dx[sin(x)] = cos(x), d⁄dx[cos(x)] = -sin(x), d⁄dx[-sin(x)] = -cos(x), d⁄dx[-cos(x)] = -sin(x).

    d⁄dx[tan(x)] = sec2(x), d⁄dx[cot(x)] = -csc2(x), d⁄dx[sec(x)] = sec(x)tan(x), d⁄dx[csc(x)] = -csc(x)cot(x).

    Other Function Derivatives

    d⁄dx[loga(x)] = 1⁄x, d⁄dx[ex] = ex.


Chain Rule

  • The chain rule involves two functions f(x) is considered the "outer" function and g(x) is the inner function.

    The formula looks like: f(g(x)) = f'(g(x)) times g'(x).

    What this means is that you take the derivative of f(x) first and leave g(x) the same inside of f(x) and then you multiply by the derivative of g(x).

    Example: sin(3x). In this example f(x) = sin(x) and g(x) = 3x. f'(x) = cos(x) and g'(x) = 3. The answer is: 3cos(3x).


Product Rule

  • The product rule is used for two functions being multiplied together.

    The formula looks like: d⁄dx[f(x)g(x)] = f'(x)g(x) + f(x)g'(x).

    The derivative of f is multiplied by g and added to f multiplied by the derivative of g.

    Example: f(x) = x2, f'(x) = 2x, g(x) = ex, g'(x) = ex. d⁄dx[x2ex] = (2x)(ex) + (x2)(ex).


Quotient Rule

  • The quotient rule involves two functions that are divided.

    The formula looks like: d⁄dx[f(x)⁄g(x)] = g(x)f'(x) - f(x)g'(x)⁄g(x)2.

    Example: f(x) = cos(x), g(x) = x2. d⁄dx[cos(x)⁄x2] = 2xcos(x) + sin(x)x2⁄x4.


Derivative of Exponentials

  • The formula for the derivative of exponentials is for exponentials other then ex because it's derivative is itself.

    The formula looks like: d⁄dx[ax] = axln(a).

    Example: d⁄dx[2x] = 2xln(2).
